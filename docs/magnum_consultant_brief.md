# The Consultant's Brief

Everything else in this project (`magnum_ecosystem.json`, `magnum_strategic_commitments.json`, `magnum_correlated_timeline.json`, the financials CSVs) is real, sourced fact — what TMICC itself has disclosed, said, or done. This document is different on purpose: it's analysis and judgment built *on top of* those facts, not a restatement of them. Every gap, connection, and recommendation below cites the real evidence behind it — but the synthesis itself is this project's own, not TMICC's stated position. Structured data lives in `data/raw/magnum_consultant_brief.json`.

## Why this exists

The other tabs answer "what is true about TMICC." This one answers the question a strategist actually needs answered: "given everything that's true, what should someone advising TMICC aim for, and where does an outside partner plausibly fit?" That's the back-tracing exercise the Strategy tab started — this is where it gets pulled together across all four research areas at once, instead of staying scattered.

## Top gaps — what's not known, and why it matters

1. **The Strategic Priorities Performance Multiplier's actual contents** (0-150% of bonus) — known to exist, not known what it measures. Matters because it's a real second lever on executive incentives that no outside party can currently verify points at the right things.
2. **India's new factories have no disclosed locations, timeline, or capital cost.** The CEO has openly named this build-out as the cause of India's weak profitability — without a timeline, there's no way to model when that margin drag ends.
3. **No disclosed financial exposure figure for the Ben & Jerry's litigation** — not even a "not material" statement with a number. Two claims survived concerning $5m in missed payments, plus unknown ongoing legal/reputational cost.
4. **Whether "Frontline First" is AMEA-specific or headed for a wider rollout** — a newly-surfaced named model, found in exactly one source so far.
5. **No named cocoa/vanilla/packaging-material trading-house supplier anywhere** (carried over from the ecosystem doc) — a real limit on assessing supply-chain concentration risk.

## Connected dots

- **AMEA's headline 22.9% margin likely blends a strong mature business with a currently-dilutive India.** India only entered the reporting perimeter in Q2 2026, so its capex-driven drag isn't even fully reflected yet in the FY2025 figure — a real risk that future AMEA margin softening gets misread as regional weakness rather than a new-market absorption effect.
- **The productivity programme's mature-market savings are plausibly funding growth-market capex.** €70m of the €90m H1 2026 savings came from supply chain — including the named "US end-to-end supply chain reset" — while capex flows into India's cold-chain build-out. TMICC doesn't say one funds the other explicitly, but that's the practical shape of it.
- **Two 2026 governance-friction events are really one story.** The Ben & Jerry's board dispute (independence from TMICC) and the 22.63% AGM dissent on the new executive incentive plan (alignment between leadership and shareholders) are legally unconnected — but both are real 2026 instances of a stakeholder group pushing back on how TMICC's leadership exercises control.
- **TMICC's fastest growth is expanding its own biggest climate liability.** The 3-million-cabinet fleet (already flagged as TMICC's largest emissions source, and not even TMICC-owned) is being expanded specifically in India, Pakistan, China, and Mexico — exactly the markets driving AMEA's growth. This tension with the stated Net Zero 2050 ambition isn't reconciled anywhere in what TMICC has disclosed.
- **The GLP-1 hedge is real but geographically incomplete.** Yasso and Breyers Carb Smart are named specifically as the Americas' answer to weight-loss-drug demand risk — no equivalent is named for Europe or AMEA, which may be reasonable (lower current penetration) or may be an unaddressed gap as adoption spreads.

## Recommendations — what to aim for

1. **Report a like-for-like "core AMEA" margin excluding India's build-out phase**, alongside the blended figure — protects the market from misreading a deliberate investment drag as underlying weakness. *(Partner angle: financial reporting/segment-disclosure advisory.)*
2. **Attach a stated end-date or capital cap to the India cold-chain build-out.** The cause of the margin drag is already public; naming when it resolves would materially de-risk the medium-term margin commitment's credibility. *(Partner angle: cold-chain infrastructure planning, capex financing/leasing.)*
3. **Engage top shareholders (BlackRock, Goldman Sachs, Fidelity, Allan & Gill Gray Foundation) on governance as one unified narrative**, not two separate issues (Ben & Jerry's + the pay-plan dissent), ahead of the next AGM. *(Partner angle: investor relations / governance consulting.)*
4. **Disclose a real financial exposure estimate for the Ben & Jerry's litigation**, even a "not material" figure — removes a currently-invisible modeling risk. *(Partner angle: legal/financial disclosure advisory.)*
5. **Publish a credible bridge between the cabinet-fleet expansion and the Net Zero 2050 ambition.** Leaving this unreconciled while actively expanding the fleet is a growing credibility gap, not a future one. *(Partner angle: sustainability/decarbonization consulting, low-carbon refrigeration technology.)*
6. **Confirm and communicate whether "Frontline First" is headed for Americas/Europe rollout** — a real near-term catalyst worth clarifying rather than leaving as an open question. *(Partner angle: field-execution/retail-execution technology.)*

## How to use this honestly

Every recommendation above traces back to a specific gap or connected dot, which in turn traces back to a real, cited fact elsewhere in this project. If you disagree with a recommendation, check the fact it's built on — the disagreement is with the analysis, not with something TMICC said. None of this should ever be quoted as "TMICC says" or "TMICC plans to" — it's what this project's own synthesis suggests TMICC *should* consider, nothing more.
